Technical Explanations

Source of Energy

The source is geothermal heat stored in subsurface reservoirs accessible via hot-water wells. Heat originates from the Earth's internal thermal gradient and possibly local magmatic or hydrothermal activity; wells tap hot fluids or steam trapped in permeable rock.

Conversion Process

Hot fluid (steam or high-temperature water) is brought to the surface through production wells. Depending on resource temperature: (a) high-temperature steam drives a conventional turbine-generator (flash or dry-steam plant); (b) moderate-temperature hot water (<180°C) transfers heat via a heat exchanger to a low-boiling working fluid in a binary (Organic Rankine Cycle) plant—this vapor drives a turbine and is subsequently condensed. After heat extraction, cooled geothermal fluid is reinjected to sustain reservoir pressure (closed-loop management).

Output / Utilization

The main output is continuous baseload electricity delivered to the grid or local microgrid. Waste heat can be cascaded for district heating, greenhouse heating, aquaculture, or industrial processes, improving overall efficiency.

Real-World Examples

Hellisheiði Power Station

Location: Iceland

Capacity: 303 MW

One of the world's largest geothermal power plants, utilizing Iceland's abundant geothermal resources for clean electricity generation.

The Geysers

Location: California, USA

Capacity: 1,517 MW

The largest geothermal field in the world, providing clean energy to Northern California through multiple power plants.

Larderello Geothermal Complex

Location: Italy

Capacity: 769 MW

The world's first geothermal power plant, operational since 1913, demonstrating the long-term viability of geothermal energy.
